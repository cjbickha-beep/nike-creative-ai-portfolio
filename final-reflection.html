FINAL_FILE_01 / LOOKING BACK

THE PROCESS
BECAME THE
PROJECT.

M1 → M5
ARCHIVED

What began as a marketing project about Nike eventually became an equally important lesson in how I work with AI. Across each milestone, I moved from using AI mainly to understand research and organize information to using it for image generation, campaign development, web design, debugging, citation management, and full HTML/CSS development.

The final website is not the result of one successful prompt. It came from continuously correcting outputs, reorganizing source material, refining my visual direction, changing platforms, rewriting prompts, checking citations, identifying hallucinations, and learning when I needed to tell the AI exactly what was wrong instead of assuming it understood my intention.

The campaign stayed centered on people throughout that process. The technology changed. The workflow changed. The prompts changed. The visual system became more specific. The core idea did not: Built by Movement. Bound by Community.

MILESTONE 02 RESEARCH / AI SOURCE CONTROL
ARCHIVE_M2_REFLECTION.TXT ORIGINAL
WORKFLOW REFLECTION / MILESTONE 2

MY IDEAS.
NOT THE AI'S.

This milestone was incredibly difficult but also really fun from a marketing perspective. Notebook was a very good program to use because it did not generate any completely new information when it came to the creative aspects of this assignment, it only reworded or explained sources that I put into the system. Additionally, I was able to tell it my ideas for the creative aspects of the marketing pitch such as color scheme, community engagement ideas, slogans, etc. and have it continue to use it to inform later generations for each slide deliverable. The biggest issues that I ran into was it remembering that I wanted to use my ideas, not the ideas that ChatGPT and Gemini came up with during my Module 2 assignment, because I uploaded those transcripts to the AI to use for my market comparison research. It kept emphasizing warm lighting and earth tones as apart of my visual focus, when I never wanted to use that for my pitch. The best thing that the Notebook did was compile research I did and make it useable and readable for the slides. However, ChatGPT and Gemini were my most used for research. When I used my AI rubric for each, they both had issues including their sources for any generation they added. However, Notebook always cited the exact source it had pulled the information from.

ARCHIVE STATUS PRESERVED / M2
SHIFT /

I started separating useful AI organization from AI-generated creative direction that did not belong to my campaign.

MILESTONE 03 IMAGE GENERATION / PROMPT REFINEMENT
ARCHIVE_M3_REFLECTION.TXT ORIGINAL
WORKFLOW REFLECTION / MILESTONE 3

REFINE THE
INITIAL PROMPT.

During the image generation process, I continuously refined my initial prompt as opposed to adding changes to each generated image. That way, I could get a new image with a natural integration of any added changes. I organized my prompts starting with my prompting goal, my image guidelines, my lighting and angle details, and then my restrictions. My iterations were pretty consistent (which was a welcome change after my technology issues). Additionally, the nature of the content I was generating was meant to be candid, so for the images the most important details was the lighting and trying to make the computer generated image look as natural as possible. Gemini did not do a great job at generating natural looking images and had an incredibly hard time making the changes or restrictions I requested. ChatGPT gave incredibly detailed and realistic results with very little need for changes after the initial prompts. Most of the hallucinations I encountered was when I was requesting help from Notebook for my caption and headline ideas. For example, it generated a caption that said “no workout routine is that serious” which is kind of the opposite value that a sports apparel company should be pushing. According to my AI rubric, Gemini scored around a ¾ , with it losing points for not being able to generate information accurate to what was prompted. ChatGPT scored a 4/4, it listened to all of my criteria and constraints and was consistent with my aesthetic plan.

ARCHIVE STATUS PRESERVED / M3
LESSON /

Prompt structure became a creative tool: goal, image guidelines, lighting and angle, then restrictions.

MILESTONE 05 WEB DEVELOPMENT / CARGO / DEBUGGING
ARCHIVE_M5_REFLECTION.TXT FINAL WEBDEV
WORKFLOW REFLECTION / MILESTONE 5

TRIAL + ERROR
BECAME A SYSTEM.

This milestone was definitely the most challenging so far of all of the milestones. Even though I started really early, I still found myself having to constantly backtrack and start over. I thought I did a good job at getting my drive organized beforehand with the proper markdown files, but ended up having to spend a lot of time going back and redoing the image chart due to switching between sites, the AI not understanding what I wanted from it, and having the image names not be clear enough for ChatGPT to understand. I made sure my site brief and slide deck were markdown files, zipped my images, and tried having Chat create an image chart for the original google sites template. Unfortunately, I am still having IT issues with my student ChatGPT account so it wasn’t able to access my Google Drive. Then, even when I did get the image chart ChatGPT refused to add the images to the code and was instead using placeholders for all of the images. Then, when I finally got it so Chat was using the images within the Google site, it stopped generating consistent aesthetics that followed the theme of my campaign. At this point I was completely exasperated with this process and I had barely started.

Then, someone overheard me talking about the project with my friend and told me that she recommended using Cargo instead of Google sites. After some back and forth I decided to completely start over and transfer to Cargo in order to make the rest of this process a lot easier. Then I had to rework my prompts to tell ChatGPT that I needed individual CSS and HTML codes, to not wrap the text, etc. because it wouldn’t let go of the old formatting for the Google site. Then, I had to reupload all of my sources and redo the image chart as well. This also took a lot of work because ChatGPT just kept giving me faulty code until I said, “This is wrong”.

The biggest issue I ran into was ChatGPT continuously affirming that it could do something when it could not. All of the actual information that it was putting from my deck and brief was correct, it wasn’t making anything up, but it failed the “consistency” part of my rubric. It would constantly contradict itself so that it could tell me, “Yes I can do that.” Finally, I was able to change my prompts so that it was creating consistent, aesthetic, and relevant pages which only required 1 or 2 tweaks per generation. I feel like even though this part of the process was really difficult, I am finally getting good at learning what works and what doesn’t work. I have to be far more deliberate than I initially thought and continuously ask the AI if it is truly able to accomplish the tasks I ask of it.

I also asked the AI to rate itself using my rubric two times for the “Research” page. The first generation of the page didn’t include source likes or any cited sources at all, which is obviously a big issue. Then, I learned how to get the AI to only rewrite specific parts of the code to include those clickable links, then I had it rate itself again to which it obviously got a much higher rating. The coding aspect of this project was initially brutal. The google sites format was not working for me whatsoever and only using the html code was making the website look amateur at best. Once I started using Cargo and worked with the AI to come up with a system and prompting strategy that worked for both of us, I found the coding to be far more fun and I was even able to change some of the code myself.

The biggest challenge truly was the back and forth of having to continuously change my original files to fit the new format or the new ideas I was having along the way. Though I tried to be as prepared as possible, this was a process of trial and error. Overall, I am proud of myself for putting in the effort to making a site that is interactive and as close to my idea as it can possibly be.

LESSON_01

I have to be far more deliberate than I initially thought. Platform, format, visual rules, exclusions, source hierarchy, and the exact part of the output that should change all matter.

LESSON_02

FINAL_FILE_09 PERSONAL REFLECTION / COMPLETE

THE FINAL TAKEAWAY /

I DIDN'T
JUST BUILD
A WEBSITE.

I learned how to build a working relationship with the technology I was using.

At the beginning of the project, I was worried about understanding the milestones and learning the vocabulary behind a large company like Nike. By the end, I was making decisions about research hierarchy, visual consistency, AI hallucinations, prompt structure, image generation, source verification, HTML, CSS, platform limitations, and interactive web design.

The hardest part was not simply learning how to prompt. It was learning that I could not assume the AI understood what I meant just because it responded confidently. I had to define what success looked like, compare the output to that definition, identify exactly what was wrong, and then make the next prompt more specific.

The switch from Google Sites to Cargo represents that change in the project. Instead of continuing with a format that was not giving me the level of visual control I wanted, I started over. That created more work, but it also gave me a website that feels much closer to the original campaign idea and gave me enough familiarity with the code that I could begin changing pieces myself.

The process was trial and error, but the trial and error eventually became a system. The final portfolio is more than a collection of campaign deliverables. It is an archive of how the campaign, the website, my prompts, and my own understanding of AI developed together.
